# Claims and artifacts

This page maps the article "How High Must a Mutation Path Climb?" (TODO-ARTICLE-URL) to this repository: section by section, then claim by claim. For each claim it gives the script, the stored output and the command, and, in the last column of each table, what the records of this repository's scheduler say about it. Stored outputs are in the data archive ([DATA.md](DATA.md)) and unpack into `experiments/`. Commands run from `experiments/` with `python -I` and the interpreter of `experiments/requirements.txt`; checking scripts are called as `../verify/NAME.py`.

Run times and memory are those the original logs or notes record, on a desktop with an 8-core AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D and 64 GB of memory, unless a row says **re-run here**; then the command was run again on 17 November 2026 on the same machine, from the tree of this repository with the archive unpacked, and the result is stated. **Not re-run** means exactly that: the stored output is the original one.

Support labels are the article's: *proof by hand*, *computer-assisted proof*, *computational*, *conjecture*. "Article" numbers are the article's; "paper" numbers are those of [paper/mutation-barriers.pdf](paper/mutation-barriers.pdf); "note" numbers are those of [docs/](docs/README.md).

## Tasks and records

The tables of the three sections after the next one are generated: their rows are in [claims/claims.toml](claims/claims.toml), and `make tables` writes them here. Every column but the last is the row as it was written by hand. In those columns **re-run here** refers to the manual reruns of 17 November 2026 described above.

The last column, *Re-run under the scheduler*, is computed from the records in [records/](records/). A claim that a program can check has tasks: computations with one answer each, defined by the batch files in [batches/](batches/) and run by the scheduler under time and memory limits ([README.md](README.md#tasks-batches-and-records)). The column reads:

- **all N tasks ok**, when every task of the claim has a record and each computed value agrees with the predicted one, with the day and the commit of the records;
- the count of tasks by status otherwise: `ok`, `mismatch`, `unsupported` (the task's method cannot compute it), `timeout`, `memory`, `error`, `crashed`, and `not re-run` for tasks without a record;
- **not re-run**, when no task of the claim has a record. The stored output named in the row is then the only support, and it is the original output, not a run of this repository;
- **no task**, with the reason, when nothing in the batch files computes the claim.

"ok" is a statement about the tasks, not about the claim: the tasks agree with their predicted values at the parameters of the batch files, which are often a small part of the range the claim covers, and for a proof by hand they are numerical support only. [claims/RECORDS.md](claims/RECORDS.md) lists every task of every claim with its predicted value, the stored line that carries the original number, and its record. A predicted value is a closed form of the article, a number the article prints, or a count copied from a stored result; [claims/expected.json](claims/expected.json) holds the last two kinds and is rebuilt from the data archive by `tools/expected.py`.

What the tasks do not cover:

- **The join of P⁺_t for 65 ≤ t ≤ 80, by search.** `batches/long-pplus.toml` has been run. The C search computed the barrier and the quivers below it for every t ≤ 64. From t = 65 the barrier is above 2³¹ − 1 and the C search, which holds entries in 32 bits, cannot compute the task: those 32 tasks end `unsupported`. The second enumerator of note 17 (`verify/review17.py plain`) counted the quivers below the barrier for every t ≤ 80. So for 65 ≤ t ≤ 80 no search finds the join itself: the tasks there are the route, replayed, and the count of the quivers below the barrier with P⁺_t the only low one, by one program, as in the original runs.
- **Conjecture 5.5 by a second program.** `batches/long-conj55.toml` has been run: the original program (`n4_conj55.py run`, `resolve`, `summary`) reproduces its per-height table, its totals and its split by certificate for heights up to 18. That is the program under check, run again. The separately written recount to height 11 and the check of note 19 to height 10 were not run again; the tasks `conj55/h=…` recompute the split by enumeration for heights up to 6 only.
- **The other long runs.** `batches/long-qa.toml` and `long-section-13.toml` describe original long runs as tasks. Neither has been run.
- **The census** (Section 8): the enumeration of all quivers of height at most 14 and of the 788,026 classes is not a task. The commands are in notes 8 and 10.
- **Section 13 from scratch.** The task `census/decided-classes` runs the counting script of note 23 on the stored outputs of the core and tube programs. It recounts; it regenerates no core and no certificate.
- **Hand proofs, and statements about the original checks** (which program was compared with which): these have no task.
- **Negative controls.** `batches/negative-control.toml` holds tasks whose predicted values are wrong on purpose; they must end `mismatch`, and they count for no claim.

## Known discrepancies

Recorded as they are; the paper has not been changed.

1. **The Q_a route at a = 12: 14,820 or 14,824 mutations.** Theorem 10.1 gives (2a + 2)(4a² − a + 6) = 14,820 at a = 12, and `np_rowwalk_family.log` records a route of 14,820 mutations. The search log `owf_barrier_summary.log` lists 14,824 in the column "L" of its first table and 14,820 in its second. They are two routes: 14,824 is the route that the generic minimax search finds, which is the theorem's route plus 4 mutations for even a ≥ 4 (plus 6 for odd a and for a = 2); 14,820 is the theorem's route, which is also what the structure-aware attacker returns ([note 21](docs/21-barriers-and-the-mutation-path-owf.md) §3.1 and the correction under its cost table; [note 23](docs/23-checks-of-notes-20-to-22.md)). Both have height 6,496.
2. **Is Theorem 13.1, first statement, new?** The paper (after its Theorem 6.1) calls items (1) and (3) reformulations of Warkentin's and Ervin's results. The working provenance record kept with the draft (not included here) says the paper "claims as new only form (1), the census use, the tube certificates and Theorem 6.6". The two disagree about item (1). The article follows the paper. Whether item (1) is new enough to state as a result is one of the questions the article leaves to a specialist.
3. **Was the Q_a state table re-derived?** The paper says of the route of Theorem 4.7 that "it has not been re-derived independently". The provenance record says that one reading of the draft did re-derive the table by hand and by integer mutation for a = 2 to 20, and that the paper "does not yet cite this". The article reports both. The scripts of that reading are not in this repository ([above](#what-is-not-stored)).
4. **Theorem numbers.** The article, the paper and the notes number statements differently (article Theorem 7.2 = paper Theorem 3.4 = note 15, Theorem 15.13). The tables above give all three where it matters.
5. **1.833 and 1.834.** The census maximum of death/birth² is 66/36 = 1.8333…. The article's text gives 1.833 and one of its figure claims 1.834; [note 11](docs/11-n4-invariants-descent-joins.md) and the paper's §3.3 use 1.834.
6. **Notes that state a result more firmly than the paper and the article.** The notes were written before the paper, and some of their summary lines were not brought down to the paper's wording. Where they differ, the paper and the article are the later reading. Found by searching the 19 ordinary notes for "proved for every", "proved for all", "is proved", "are proved", "What is proved", and for every statement header of the form "Theorem N.k [I …]", and comparing each hit with the article's status table and Sections 7 to 13:
   - **The Q_a route.** [Note 18](docs/18-five-vertices-cubic-barrier.md) §1 item 1 and its table in §9 say "the route is proved for every w", and Theorem 18.4 carries the plain label [I]. The paper prints the route as a sketch and says the derivation "has not been re-derived independently"; the article says "derived by hand, printed as a sketch; replayed for a ≤ 48".
   - **R_b and Q_t.** [Note 15](docs/15-rank-2-orbits-and-the-constant-2.md) heads Theorems 15.6 and 15.13 with the plain label [I] and says "What is proved: a new lower bound"; [note 10](docs/10-n4-quadratic-bound.md)'s follow-up says "proved for every even b ≥ 6". The lower bounds are computer-assisted proofs (43 and 30 polynomial claims certified by a program), as note 15 §5 itself explains, and the article labels them so.
   - **Statements headed "Theorem" that are computations or computer-assisted.** Theorem 16.5 and Theorem 18.5 ("[I, by exhaustive computation]"), Theorem 20.4 ("[I, by exhaustive computation]"), Theorem 16.6 ("[I, computer-assisted]") and Theorem 9.1 ("[I, computer-assisted proof]"). The article calls the searches for Q_a a Proposition "(computational)". For Theorem 9.1 the difference runs the other way: note 12 §4 later gave a proof by hand, which is what the paper and the article use.
   - **The tube lemma and Theorem 13.1 of the article.** [Note 22](docs/22-tube-lemma-and-its-limits.md) §1 says "The lemma is proved", and [note 14](docs/14-opposite-quivers-forks-and-cores.md) states Theorem 14.1 as "[I, from F1–F4]". The paper gives both proofs as sketches, note 23 added two hypotheses to Theorem 22.5, and the paper calls two items of its Theorem 6.1 reformulations of known results.
   - **258,363 or 239,507 classes.** Note 14 §9 still says its bound on pockets "is proved for 239,507 classes"; the correction to 258,363 (note 19 §6.3) is quoted in its §1 and §5 only. 258,363 is the number in `n4_opposite_pockets.log`, the paper and the article.
   - **Joins inside one orbit, within w² + w.** Note 15 states this as Proposition 15.3 with a proof; the paper states it without proof, and the article says so. Here the note says more than the paper prints, not more than is claimed.
7. **"Every pocket" in the thin-summit observation.** The article's last section says every census pocket born at height ≤ 9 has a join of least height on which each quiver at the top height has an entry of absolute value at most 2 (note 15, Observation 15.9). `n4_doors_thin.log` also reports a stricter variant, in which every quiver above the birth level must have such an entry: that one holds for 30,523 of the 30,527 pockets. The two statements are different and both are in the log.
